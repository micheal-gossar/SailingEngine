UNDERWATER MEDIA RENDERER CLASS DESIGN
======================================

Class
-----

    UnderwaterMediaRenderer

Purpose
-------

Render view-dependent participating seawater, the moving waterline, underwater
lighting and shadows, volumetric caustics, depth/color attenuation, suspended
matter, and physically consistent transitions through the air-water interface.

Public API
----------

    Result<void, OceanRenderError> Initialize(
        const UnderwaterMediaDescription&);
    void SetWaterBodies(span<const WaterOpticalVolume>);
    void AddPreSurfacePasses(RenderGraphBuilder&, const OceanRenderSnapshot&,
                             const ViewFamily&);
    void AddCompositePasses(RenderGraphBuilder&, const OceanRenderSnapshot&,
                            const ViewFamily&);
    UnderwaterMediaStatistics Statistics() const;

Key data structures
-------------------

WaterOpticalVolume stores absorption and scattering spectra, phase-function
parameters, particulate/turbidity profile, chlorophyll proxy, dissolved color,
density layer, bounds, and priority. MediaFroxel stores transmittance, in-
scattered radiance, emissive bioluminescence channel, and validity. Waterline
classification stores per-pixel entry/exit depth and medium ID. CausticField
stores irradiance, receiver depth range, confidence, temporal generation, and
energy normalization. SuspendedMatterTile stores visual concentration and flow.

Algorithms and models
---------------------

Classify camera and rays against the same displaced ocean surface used by
OceanSurfaceRenderer. Reconstruct water entry/exit positions using surface and
opaque depths, including partially submerged pixels and near-plane crossings.
Integrate Beer-Lambert extinction and single scattering in logarithmic froxels;
use a validated energy-preserving multiple-scattering approximation for high
albedo water. Sample sun/sky shadowing and local lights without applying fog
twice. Optical profiles derive from environmental water type and ecosystem
state but interpolate slowly and remain bounded.

Generate caustics by tracing/reprojecting a bounded distribution of refracted
sun rays or wave-slope wavefronts onto terrain/objects. Normalize deposited
irradiance to incident transmitted solar energy, filter spatially, and reject
history on wave/origin/receiver changes. Coarse analytic focusing is the
fallback; an unbounded bright animated texture is not. Shadow and caustic
visibility use bathymetry/scene depth. Suspended sediment/plankton advects with
coastal/current fields for visual concentration and modulates scattering.

Caustic history is stored in receiver world-cell coordinates, not screen space,
and carries sun direction, wave/surface generation, bathymetry/receiver
generation, origin generation, sample count, first/second moments, and
confidence. Reprojection uses previous/current surface ray footprints and
receiver motion; disocclusion, wet/dry change, large Jacobian/phase change, sun
motion, camera cut, or LOD replacement rejects history. Variance-guided clamping
uses current unbiased neighborhood bounds and cannot preserve energy from an
obsolete bright focus. A short decorrelated fallback window prevents frozen
caustics when history is invalid.

Froxel temporal accumulation similarly reprojects world path segments and
compares medium id, entry/exit depth, optical coefficient generation, and
motion. Underwater terrain/animal motion vectors and waterline coverage prevent
air/water ghosting. Jitter sequences are deterministic by view and frame but are
not simulation state.

Underwater terrain, habitats, animals, particles, and vessels shade into the
same medium contract: surfaces output in-scattered-free radiance and path depth;
the media composite applies transmittance once. Total internal reflection and
water-surface underside are supplied by OceanSurfaceRenderer.

Update rates, threading, and GPU ownership
-----------------------------------------

Optical environment profiles update at 1-2 Hz. Froxel integration and waterline
classification run every view/frame; caustics update at 15-60 Hz depending on
quality and temporal reprojection. Suspended matter compute runs at 5-15 Hz.
Renderer owns history/froxel/caustic persistent images. RenderGraph owns
transients and cross-queue barriers. CPU owns profile metadata only; no gameplay
state depends on visual media.

Lifecycle and dependencies
--------------------------

Initialize after lighting/shadow, terrain depth, OceanSurfaceRenderer interface
formats, and ocean snapshot availability. Optical volumes stream by ocean cell
and switch at frame boundaries. Camera medium changes invalidate relevant
history. Device recreation clears histories but retains CPU profiles. See
..\rendering\27_sky_atmosphere.txt, ..\rendering\45_lighting_shadow.txt,
..\world\26_terrain_system.txt, 78_ocean_surface_renderer.txt,
80_ocean_effects_system.txt, and 83_underwater_habitat_renderer.txt.

Failure and error behavior
--------------------------

Errors include InvalidOpticalCoefficients, OverlappingMediumPriority,
MissingSurfaceDepth, UnsupportedFroxelFormat, CausticBudgetExceeded,
SnapshotMismatch, TemporalGenerationMismatch, CausticEnergyViolation, and
GpuAllocationFailed. Invalid profiles fall back to a
named calibrated open-ocean profile and low-confidence diagnostic. Missing
caustics does not disable attenuation. If waterline classification is invalid,
conservatively render the camera's current medium and disable cross-interface
refraction for that pixel.

Deterministic and testing strategy
----------------------------------

Reference tests compare homogeneous-medium transmittance to analytic
Beer-Lambert, single-scatter slabs, phase-function normalization, waterline ray
intersections, Snell entry/exit, total internal reflection, energy of caustic
deposition, shadowed caustics, overlapping volumes, temporal rejection, camera
crossing, origin shift, and reversed-Z/MSAA variants. Fixed GPU captures test
surface/media ordering and no double fog. Profile cooking has spectral golden
tables.
Temporal sequences include moving receiver/animal, shore wet/dry, bathymetry
LOD swap, spectrum transition, sun motion, camera teleport, origin rebase, and
disocclusion; metrics report ghost half-life, variance, rejected-history ratio,
and deposited-power error rather than a single still image.

Performance and scalability
---------------------------

Use clustered froxels, empty-space skipping, depth-bounded integration,
checkerboard/temporal caustics, half resolution with bilateral upsample,
view-shared optical atlases, and sparse suspended-matter tiles. Quality scales
froxel depth/screen resolution, light count, caustic rays/update rate, and
multiple scattering. Preserve extinction coefficients and waterline accuracy
at every tier. Profile compute/graphics overlap, pixels submerged, froxel fill,
light samples, caustic variance, history rejection, and memory.

Integration points
------------------

Environment forcing supplies thermohaline/water type. Ecosystem supplies
bounded chlorophyll/plankton and optional bioluminescence fields. Terrain and
habitat renderers supply receivers/occlusion. Surface renderer consumes medium
transmission for refraction and reflection. Effects supplies bubbles/sediment.
Audio uses separate acoustics in file 84 and must not infer sound from visual
fog. GraphicsQualityManager selects validated policy from file 86.

Engine-wide integration contract
--------------------------------

Normative presentation-only media/history owner. P14/P15 consume immutable
physical/profile snapshots; optical visual state never owns or mutates ocean,
ecosystem, terrain, audio, or gameplay. Persistent images retire through the
one central GpuRetirementQueue after GpuCompletionSet; RenderGraph owns
transient states/barriers. See ..\CANONICAL_CONVENTIONS.txt,
..\rendering\INDEX.txt, and 88_ocean_gpu_execution_sync.txt.
